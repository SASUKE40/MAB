## K-armed & contextual

Pick one of K arms each round and see only its reward. Includes adversarial, non-stationary, rested and contextual environments.

## Best-arm identification

Pure exploration: sample arms to name the best one (or the top m) — as fast as possible at confidence 1 − δ, or as reliably as possible within a budget of T pulls.

Environments: **Bernoulli, one clear best** (`bern-easy`), **Bernoulli, close gaps** (`bern-close`), **Gaussian, evenly spaced** (`gauss-arith`), **Gaussian, one good arm** (`gauss-one`), **Linear (Soare et al.)** (`linear`), **Bernoulli, top-3** (`topm`).

### Baseline

Uniform round-robin sampling, stopped by the GLR rule. Every adaptive method should beat it.

| Algorithm | id | Rule | Guarantee | Defaults | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Uniform sampling | `uniform` | aₜ = t mod K; stop when min_{b≠â} Z_{â,b}(t) > β(t,δ) | E[τ] = O(K Δ_min⁻² ln(1/δ)) |  | Round-robin over all arms with GLR stopping. The fair yardstick: every adaptive method should need fewer samples. |

### Elimination

| Algorithm | id | Rule | Guarantee | Defaults | Notes |

| Successive Elimination | `se` | drop a if #{b: LCB_b > UCB_a} ≥ m,  c_r = s√(2 ln(4Kr²/δ)/r) | O(Σₐ Δₐ⁻² ln(K/(δΔₐ))) |  | Even-Dar et al. | Median Elimination | `me` | round l: pull each survivor (2s)²·(4/ε_l²)·ln(3/δ_l) times, keep the top half; ε_{l+1} = ¾ε_l, δ_{l+1} = δ_l/2 | O((K/ε²) ln(1/δ)) samples; returns an ε-good arm w.p. 1 − δ | ε = 0.3 | Even-Dar et al. | GIFA (LUCB-type gap index) | `gifa` | B_{i,j} = μ̂ⱼ − μ̂ᵢ + s√(2(1/nᵢ+1/nⱼ)β(t,δ)); (c, b) = argmax_{i∈J, j∉J} B; pull the less-sampled; stop when max B ≤ 0 | δ-correct; O(H₁ ln(H₁/δ))-type, like LUCB / m-LinGapE |  | One instance of the gap-index focused algorithms of Reda, Kaufmann & Delahaye-Duriez (2021): a paired (not per-arm) gap index between the empirical top m and the rest, LUCB-style pair selection and the "largest variance" rule. ### Optimal allocation

Plug the empirical means into the lower-bound allocation w*(μ) and track it. Asymptotically optimal.

| Algorithm | id | Rule | Guarantee | Defaults | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Track-and-Stop (C-tracking) | `tas-c` | aₜ = argmaxₐ Σ_{s≤t} w^{ε_s}ₐ(μ̂(s)) − nₐ,  ε_s = ½(K²+s)^{-½}; GLR stop | lim_{δ→0} E[τ]/ln(1/δ) = T*(μ) (asymptotically optimal) |  | Garivier & Kaufmann (2016): compute the optimal proportions w*(μ̂) by bisection on F(y) = 1 and track their running sum, mixed with a vanishing uniform share. Weights refreshed every ~10% of pulls (lazy). |
| Track-and-Stop (D-tracking) | `tas-d` | aₜ = argmin nₐ if minₐ nₐ < √t − K/2, else argmaxₐ t·w*ₐ(μ̂) − nₐ; GLR stop | lim_{δ→0} E[τ]/ln(1/δ) = T*(μ) (asymptotically optimal) |  | Garivier & Kaufmann (2016): forced exploration keeps every arm above √t pulls; otherwise pull the arm furthest below its optimal share. Weights refreshed every ~10% of pulls (lazy). |
| Frank–Wolfe Sampling | `fws` | z = argmax_{z∈Δ} min_{b∈B_r} ⟨∇f_b(w), z − w⟩,  w = n/t, f_b = transport cost; track Σz; GLR stop | lim_{δ→0} E[τ]/ln(1/δ) = T*(μ) (asymptotically optimal) |  | Wang, Tzeng & Proutiere (2021): a Frank–Wolfe step on the non-smooth lower-bound objective using its r-subdifferential (all nearly-active rival arms). ### Top two

Each round pick a leader and a challenger and pull the leader with probability β.

| Algorithm | id | Rule | Guarantee | Defaults | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| TTTS | `ttts` | leader = argmax θ, θ ~ Π; w.p. 1−β resample until argmax θ′ ≠ leader; GLR stop | E[τ]/ln(1/δ) → T*_β(μ) (β-optimal) with GLR stopping | β = 0.5 | Russo (2016): Thompson sampling picks a leader; with probability 1 − β the posterior is resampled until another arm wins, and that challenger is pulled. | T3C | `t3c` | leader = argmax θ, θ ~ Π; challenger = argmin_{b≠leader} W(leader, b); GLR stop | E[τ]/ln(1/δ) → T*_β(μ) (β-optimal), cheaper than TTTS | β = 0.5 | Shang et al. | TTUCB | `ttucb` | leader = argmax μ̂ₐ + s√(2 ln t/nₐ); challenger = argmin W(leader, b); tracking per leader; GLR stop | asymptotically β-optimal with a non-asymptotic E[τ] bound | β = 0.5 | Jourdan & Degenne (2022): a deterministic top-two method with a UCB leader and transportation-cost challenger; the leader/challenger split is tracked per leader rather than randomised. |

### Fixed budget

| Algorithm | id | Rule | Guarantee | Defaults | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Successive Rejects | `sr` | phase k: pull survivors to n_k = ⌈(T−K)/(loḡK·(K+1−k))⌉, reject argmin μ̂ | P(err) ≤ K(K−1)/2 · exp(−(T−K)/(loḡK · H₂)) |  | Audibert & Bubeck (2010): K − 1 phases of growing length; the empirically worst survivor is rejected at the end of each phase. | Sequential Halving | `sh` | round r of ⌈log₂ K⌉: pull each survivor ⌊T/(\|S_r\|⌈log₂K⌉)⌋ times, keep the top half by round means | P(err) ≤ 3 log₂K · exp(−T/(8 H₂ log₂K)) |  | Karnin, Koren & Somekh (2013): split the budget evenly over ⌈log₂ K⌉ rounds and halve the arm set after each, judging arms only by that round's fresh samples. | SAR (Successive Accepts and Rejects) | `sar` | SR phases; at phase end accept the top arm if μ̂₍₁₎ − μ̂₍ₘ′₊₁₎ > μ̂₍ₘ′₎ − μ̂₍\|A\|₎, else reject the bottom one | P(err) ≤ 2K² · exp(−(T−K)/(8 loḡK · H⟨m⟩)) |  | Bubeck, Wang & Viswanathan (2013): for top-m, each phase either accepts the arm most clearly in the top set or rejects the one most clearly out, whichever empirical gap is larger. ## Combinatorial & ranking

Choose a list of m items out of L each round and learn from semi-bandit, cascade, click-model or total-reward feedback.

Environments: **Top-m semi-bandit** (`topm`), **Partition matroid** (`matroid`), **Cascading clicks** (`cascade`), **Dependent click model** (`dcm`), **Position-based model** (`pbm`), **Cascade with item features** (`cascade-lin`), **Adversarial full-bandit** (`adversarial`).

### Baselines

Reference points: no learning, or learning without exploration.

| Algorithm | id | Rule | Guarantee | Defaults | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Random | `random` | Aₜ ~ Uniform(feasible actions) | Θ(T) |  | Ignores feedback and plays a uniformly random feasible list. Its regret is the price of pure exploration. |
| Greedy | `greedy` | Aₜ = Oracle(ŵ)   (unseen items first; random list w.p. ε) | Θ(T) in the worst case | ε = 0 | Tries every item once, then always plays the oracle on empirical means. Locks onto lists that looked good early. |

### Stochastic semi-bandit

| Algorithm | id | Rule | Guarantee | Defaults | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CUCB | `cucb` | Aₜ = Oracle(μ̄),   μ̄ᵢ = min(1, ŵᵢ + √(c·ln t / nᵢ)) | O(Σᵢ m log T / Δᵢ)  (bounded-smoothness rewards) | c = 1.5 | Chen, Wang & Yuan (2013): clipped UCB per item fed to an exact oracle; works for any monotone reward with bounded smoothness. ## Continuum-armed & GP

Pick a point x in [0,1] (or [0,1]²) and observe f(x) + noise: Lipschitz discretization, optimistic tree search, bandit convex optimisation, Gaussian-process and safe bandits.

Environments: **Smooth unimodal (1-D)** (`smooth`), **Multimodal bumps (1-D)** (`multimodal`), **Needle in a haystack (1-D)** (`needle`), **Branin, rescaled (2-D)** (`branin`), **Concave quadratic (1-D, convex loss)** (`convex`), **Safe optimisation (1-D, h = 0.35)** (`safe`), **Contextual peak (1-D, context c)** (`contextual`).

### Baseline

No learning: shows the cost of pure exploration.

| Algorithm | id | Rule | Guarantee | Defaults | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Uniform random | `uniform` | xₜ ~ U([0,1]ᵈ) | Θ(T) |  | Samples the domain uniformly and recommends the best point it observed. Its regret is the cost of pure exploration. |

### Bandit convex optimisation

## Dueling bandits

Pick two arms each round and see only which one won. Regret is measured against the Condorcet, Copeland or Borda winner.

Environments: **Bradley–Terry (Condorcet)** (`bt`), **Condorcet with cycles** (`cyclic`), **Copeland (no Condorcet winner)** (`copeland`), **Borda ≠ Condorcet** (`borda`), **Continuous concave utility** (`continuous`).

### Baseline

Ignores the feedback; the cost of pure exploration.

| Algorithm | id | Rule | Guarantee | Defaults | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Uniform pairs | `uniform` | (i, j) ~ Uniform([K] × [K]) | Θ(T) |  | Duels two arms drawn uniformly at random every round and never learns. Its regret is the price of not adapting. |

### Explore then exploit

Run a tournament until one arm is confidently best, then duel it against itself forever.

| Algorithm | id | Rule | Guarantee | Defaults | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Interleaved Filter | `if` | duel b̂ vs each b ∈ W in turn; drop b if P̂_b̂b − c > ½, switch b̂ ← b if P̂_b̂b + c < ½; c = √(ln(TK²)/n) | O(K log T / ε₁,₂) (total order) |  | Yue et al. (2012), IF2 with pruning: a candidate meets every survivor round-robin and is replaced by the first arm that confidently beats it. | Beat the Mean | `btm` | duel b = argmin_W n_b vs b′ ~ U(W); drop argmin P̂_b once min P̂ + c ≤ max P̂ − c, c = 3γ²√(ln(2TK)/n*) | O(γ⁷ K log T / ε²) | γ = 1 | Yue & Joachims (2011): each survivor is scored by its win rate against a random survivor (the "mean bandit"); the worst is dropped once separated, and its duels are erased from everyone's record. | SAVAGE | `savage` | duel the least-sampled undecided pair touching a candidate; drop i once ∃ j: P̂ᵢⱼ + c < ½, c = √(ln(K²T/δ)/2n) | O(K² log(KT/δ) / ε²) samples | Confidence = 0.1 | Urvoy et al. ## Bandits with knapsacks

Every pull earns a reward and spends limited resources; the run ends when any budget is exhausted. Compared against the LP-relaxation optimum.

Environments: **One resource, tight budget** (`one`), **Two resources** (`two`), **Shifting (adversarial-ish)** (`shift`), **Contextual, linear rewards & costs** (`ctx`).

### Cost-unaware & greedy baselines

Ignore the budget, or chase reward per unit of cost without planning.

| Algorithm | id | Rule | Guarantee | Defaults | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| UCB1 (ignores costs) | `ucb1` | aₜ = argmaxₐ μ̂ₐ + c·√(ln t / nₐ) | Ω(T) in BwK: spends the budget on the best-reward arm | Exploration = 1.414 | Plain UCB1 on rewards. | Reward-per-cost UCB | `ucb-ratio` | aₜ = argmaxₐ UCB(μₐ) / maxⱼ LCB(cₐⱼ) | none in general (greedy on a ratio; ignores the time constraint) | Exploration = 1 | Greedy bang-per-buck: optimistic reward divided by optimistic (low) consumption of the scarcest resource. ### Selfish baseline

| Algorithm | id | Rule | Guarantee | Defaults | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Simplified: fixed phase lengths, content counts over the last half of each GoT phase, and a sharper acceptance ε^{κ(u_max−u)} (κ = 3; the paper's κ = 1 needs far longer horizons to mix). |

### Federated

No collisions: agents learn the same arms in parallel and periodically pool their statistics.

| Algorithm | id | Rule | Guarantee | Defaults | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FedUCB (periodic sync) | `fed-ucb` | aₜᵖ = argmaxₐ (Sₐ + sₐᵖ)/(Nₐ + nₐᵖ) + c·√(ln(Mt) / (Nₐ + nₐᵖ));  every τ rounds: (S, N) += Σₚ (sᵖ, nᵖ) | O(Σ log(MT)/Δ) total + O(MK·T/τ) communication | Sync = 10, Exploration = 1.414 | Federated UCB in the spirit of Shi & Shen (2021, Fed-UCB), homogeneous case: every agent runs UCB on the server's pooled statistics plus its own unsent samples, and uploads every τ rounds. Simplified: no local/global heterogeneity and no growing exploration schedule. In collision environments it herds all agents onto the same arm. |
| FedLinUCB (periodic sync) | `fed-linucb` | aₜᵖ = argmaxₐ θ̂ₚ·xₐ + α√ln(1+Mt)·‖xₐ‖_{Vₚ⁻¹},  Vₚ = λI + V_sync + ΔVₚ;  every τ rounds V_sync += Σₚ ΔVₚ | Õ(d√(MT)) total for suitable sync schedules | Sync = 10, Confidence = 0.5 | The federated LinUCB of Dubey & Pentland (2020) without the privacy noise: agents run LinUCB on the pooled Gram matrix and response vector plus their own unsent data, and synchronize. Simplified: a fixed period τ instead of the log-det trigger. On K-armed environments the features are one-hot, which makes it a ridge-regularised FedUCB. |

## Off-policy & offline

Learn from logged contextual-bandit data you did not collect: estimate a target policy's value (estimators, scored by RMSE) or learn a new policy (learners, scored by suboptimality).

Environments: **Linear, good overlap** (`linear`), **Non-linear rewards (misspecified model)** (`misspecified`), **Low overlap (near-deterministic logging)** (`lowoverlap`), **Small sample (n ≤ 150, noisy)** (`small`).

### Importance weighting

Reweight logged rewards by π/π₀. Unbiased or nearly so, but noisy when the policies disagree.

| Algorithm | id | Rule | Guarantee | Defaults | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| IPS | `ips` | V̂ = (1/n) Σᵢ wᵢ rᵢ,  wᵢ = π(aᵢ\|xᵢ) / π₀(aᵢ\|xᵢ) | Unbiased; variance ∝ E[w² r²] / n, explodes when overlap is poor |  | Estimator. Reweights each logged reward by how much more often the target policy would have taken that action. No model, no bias, but large weights make it noisy. |
| SNIPS | `snips` | V̂ = Σᵢ wᵢ rᵢ / Σᵢ wᵢ | Bias O(1/n); usually much lower variance than IPS; invariant to reward shifts |  | Estimator. Self-normalised IPS (Swaminathan & Joachims 2015): divides by the realised sum of weights instead of n, trading a small bias for stability. |
| Clipped IPS | `cips` | V̂ = (1/n) Σᵢ min(wᵢ, M) rᵢ | Bias ≤ E[(w − M)₊ \|r\|]; variance ≤ M² E[r²] / n | Clip = 10 | Estimator. IPS with importance weights capped at M (Bottou et al. 2013): bounded variance, at the price of a bias towards the logging policy. |

### Model-based

Fit a reward model and plug it in. Low variance, biased when the model is wrong.

| Algorithm | id | Rule | Guarantee | Defaults | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DM (ridge) | `dm` | V̂ = (1/n) Σᵢ Σₐ π(a\|xᵢ) q̂(xᵢ,a),  q̂ = ridge regression per action | Low variance; bias = reward-model error, which does not vanish if the model is misspecified | Ridge = 1 | Estimator. Direct method: fit a reward model on the logged data, then average its predictions under the target policy. Ignores the propensities entirely. |

### Doubly robust & blends

Combine a reward model with importance weights to get the best of both.

| Algorithm | id | Rule | Guarantee | Defaults | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DR | `dr` | V̂ = (1/n) Σᵢ [ Σₐ π(a\|xᵢ) q̂(xᵢ,a) + wᵢ (rᵢ − q̂(xᵢ,aᵢ)) ] | Unbiased with known π₀; variance ∝ E[w² (r − q̂)²] / n, small when q̂ is good | Ridge = 1 | Estimator. Doubly robust (Dudík, Langford & Li 2011): DM plus an importance-weighted correction of the model residual. Accurate if either the model or the propensities are right. |
| MRDR | `mrdr` | DR with q̂ = argmin Σᵢ π(aᵢ\|xᵢ)(1 − π₀ᵢ)/π₀ᵢ² · (rᵢ − q(xᵢ,aᵢ))² | Unbiased; minimises the DR variance within the model class | Ridge = 1 | Estimator. More robust DR (Farajtabar, Chow & Ghavamzadeh 2018): the reward model is fitted to minimise the variance of DR rather than the squared error. Simplified: the per-sample weights of the deterministic-target derivation (as in Open Bandit Pipeline), weighted ridge. |
| SWITCH-DR | `switch-dr` | V̂ = (1/n) Σᵢ [ Σₐ π(a\|xᵢ) q̂(xᵢ,a) + 1{wᵢ ≤ τ} wᵢ (rᵢ − q̂(xᵢ,aᵢ)) ] | Minimax-optimal MSE up to constants (Wang, Agarwal & Dudík 2017) | Ridge = 1, Threshold = 0 | Estimator. Uses DR where the importance weight is at most τ and the model alone where it is larger. With τ = 0 the threshold is picked from a grid by minimising estimated variance + (σ̂ · target mass above τ)², a simplified version of the paper's tuning. |
| CAB | `cab` | V̂ = (1/n) Σᵢ [ min(wᵢ, M) rᵢ + Σₐ (π(a\|xᵢ) − M π₀(a\|xᵢ))₊ q̂(xᵢ,a) ] | Bias only from q̂ on the clipped mass; variance ≤ M² / n; sub-differentiable in π | Ridge = 1, Clip = 2 | Estimator. Continuous adaptive blending (Su, Wang, Srinath & Joachims 2019): clipped IPS, with the probability mass removed by clipping filled in by the reward model. Blends IPS and DM per action rather than switching. |

### Policy learning

Learn a new policy from the log: greedy plug-ins and counterfactual risk minimisation.

| Algorithm | id | Rule | Guarantee | Defaults | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DM-greedy | `dm-greedy` | π̂(x) = argmaxₐ q̂(x,a),  q̂ = ridge regression per action | Consistent only with a well-specified model and full coverage; overestimates its own value | Ridge = 1 | Learner. Plug-in baseline: fit the reward model and act greedily on it. Rarely logged actions with lucky estimates win the argmax (the winner's curse). |
| IPS-greedy | `ips-greedy` | π̂(x) = argmaxₐ x·θ̂ₐ,  θ̂ₐ = ridge fit of x ↦ rᵢ 1{aᵢ = a} / π₀ᵢ | Unbiased reward targets but no variance control: unreliable under poor overlap | Ridge = 1 | Learner. Regresses importance-weighted pseudo-rewards (unbiased for every action) on the context and acts greedily. Its own value estimate is the IPS estimate of the greedy policy. |
| POEM | `poem` | max_W  R̂ᴹ(π_W) − λ √(V̂ar/n) − (ρ/2)‖W‖²,  π_W(a\|x) ∝ exp(W_a·x) | CRM bound: R(π) ≥ R̂ᴹ(π) − O(√(V̂ar·C/n) + C/n) uniformly over the class | Variance = 1, Clip = 10, L2 = 0.001, Steps = 40 | Learner. Policy optimiser for exponential models (Swaminathan & Joachims 2015): counterfactual risk minimisation, i.e. clipped IPS with a sample-variance penalty, on a softmax-linear policy. Simplified: full-batch Adam on the exact objective instead of the majorise-minimise SGD. |
| SVP | `svp` | max_π  R̂_IPS(π) − λ √(V̂ar(π)/n) | Excess risk O(√(Var*·C/n) + C/n) (Maurer & Pontil 2009) | Variance = 1, Steps = 40 | Learner. Plain sample-variance penalisation (Maurer & Pontil 2009) of the unclipped IPS objective over the same softmax-linear class. POEM is SVP applied to clipped IPS with L2; the two coincide when M = ∞ and ρ = 0, so the difference here isolates clipping and regularisation. |

### Pessimism

Act on lower confidence bounds so that poorly covered actions are not chosen by luck.

| Algorithm | id | Rule | Guarantee | Defaults | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PEVI / LCB (tabular) | `pevi` | π̂(x) = argmaxₐ r̄(x,a) − β σ̂ / √n(x,a) | V* − V(π̂) = Õ(√(C*·\|X\| / n)) under single-policy coverage C* (Rashidinejad et al. 2021) | Pessimism = 1 | Learner. Pessimism (PEVI for one step, Jin, Yang & Wang 2021; LCB, Rashidinejad et al. 2021) on the tabular problem over the finite context pool: act on lower confidence bounds, so unlogged actions are never chosen. Contexts never logged fall back to the context-free K-armed LCB. |
| LinLCB | `linlcb` | π̂(x) = argmaxₐ x·θ̂ₐ − β σ̂ ‖x‖_{Aₐ⁻¹} | V* − V(π̂) = Õ(d·√(C*/n)) under single-policy coverage (Jin, Yang & Wang 2021) | Ridge = 1, Pessimism = 1 | Learner. Pessimistic ridge regression: subtract the confidence width of each action's linear model, so poorly covered actions need strong evidence. With β = 0 it is exactly DM-greedy. |
| NeuralLCB | `neurallcb` | π̂(x) = argmaxₐ f(x,a;θ̂) − β σ̂ √(gᵀ diag(Λ)⁻¹ g),  g = ∇_θ f(x,a;θ̂) | Õ(√(d̃ / n)) with effective dimension d̃ under coverage (Nguyen-Tang et al. 2022) | Pessimism = 1, Hidden = 16, SGD = 60 | Learner. Pessimism with a neural reward model (Nguyen-Tang, Gupta, Tran & Venkatesh 2022). Simplified: one ReLU hidden layer with a head per action, mini-batch Adam warm-started between refits, and a diagonal Λ = λI + Σ g gᵀ of gradient features. |

## Restless bandits

Arms are two-state Markov chains that keep moving whether pulled or not; pull M of N each round to keep as many as possible in the good state.

Environments: **Adherence (indexable)** (`adherence`), **Heterogeneous arm types** (`hetero`), **Intervention fatigue (hard)** (`fatigue`).

### Oracle reference

Knows the true transition matrices. Not a learner: it defines the benchmark.

| Algorithm | id | Rule | Guarantee | Defaults | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Whittle index (oracle) | `whittle-oracle` | pull the M arms with the largest W(sᵢ) = inf{λ : passive optimal in sᵢ with subsidy λ}, true P | 0 by definition (it is the benchmark at γ = 0.95) | Discount = 0.95 | Whittle (1988): give each arm a subsidy for staying passive; the index of a state is the subsidy that makes acting and resting equally good. With the true transitions this is the reference policy every learner is compared to. Oracle: it is told the model. |

### Learning Whittle indices

Estimate the transitions from observed state changes and plug them into the Whittle index.

| Algorithm | id | Rule | Guarantee | Defaults | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| UCWhittle (simplified) | `ucwhittle` | P̃¹ₛ = P̂¹ₛ + c√(ln t / n¹ₛ),  P̃⁰ₛ = P̂⁰ₛ − c√(ln t / n⁰ₛ);  pull top-M W(sᵢ; P̃) | Õ(√T) (episodic, Wang et al. 2023) | Confidence = 0.5 | Wang, Xiong, Liu, Jia & Tambe (2023): optimism for restless bandits — compute Whittle indices from the most favourable transitions in the confidence region. Simplified: for two-state arms the optimistic corner is taken directly (active probabilities at their upper bound, passive at their lower bound), recomputed every round instead of per episode. |
| Thompson-sampling Whittle | `ts-whittle` | P̃ₖ ~ Beta(1 + goodₖ, 1 + badₖ) for every (arm, action, state);  pull top-M W(sᵢ; P̃) | Õ(√T) Bayesian (posterior-sampling RL) | Resample = 1 | Posterior sampling for restless bandits (in the spirit of Jung & Tewari 2019 and Akbarzadeh & Mahajan 2022): draw each transition probability from its Beta posterior, compute the Whittle indices of that sampled model and act on them. Resampled every H rounds. |
| Plug-in Whittle (greedy) | `ce-whittle` | P̂ = (good + 1)/(n + 2);  pull top-M W(sᵢ; P̂) | no guarantee (certainty equivalence) |  | Certainty equivalence: plug the posterior-mean estimates into the Whittle index with no optimism. Passive transitions are learned for free, but an arm whose active effect is underestimated early may never be pulled again. |

### Baselines

Myopic, round-robin and random scheduling.

| Algorithm | id | Rule | Guarantee | Defaults | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Myopic (greedy one-step) | `myopic` | pull top-M of P̂¹(sᵢ) − P̂⁰(sᵢ) | none (ignores the future) |  | Pull the arms whose estimated chance of being good next round rises the most when pulled. Ignores that a pull now also changes where the arm will be later, which matters when states are sticky. |
| Round-robin | `round-robin` | pull arms (M(t−1) + j) mod N, j = 0..M−1 | Θ(T) |  | Cycle through the arms in a fixed order, ignoring their states. A common real-world scheduling default. |
| Random | `random` | pull a uniformly random set of M arms | Θ(T) |  | Ignores states and history. |
